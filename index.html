If you read nothing else

Ten rules every Gemba asset follows.

  1. 01Poppins for words, Geist Mono for numbers. No other fonts.
  2. 02Dark first: Void black or Obsidian grounds. Paper (#F4F1EA) for print and long reading.
  3. 03One accent per asset. Green for consulting and transformation, blue for platform and products.
  4. 04Highlight one thing in a headline: a grey lead line, or the last word in accent. Never both.
  5. 05Every claim carries a recorded number, a named industry or a real plant.
  6. 06Images must show the exact operation being discussed. Real and human, never generic stock or an obvious AI look.
  7. 07On dark grounds the logo sits on its white plate. Never recolour, stretch or rebuild it.
  8. 08Short declarative sentences with full stops. Write like a plant head briefing another plant head.
  9. 09One message, one focal point, one action per surface.
  10. 10Run the pre-publish check before anything leaves the company.

Built on the floor. Not in a slide deck.

Who we are, what we promise and how we work. Every visual and written decision in this book traces back to this page.

What "Gemba" means

"The actual place."

Gemba is a Japanese term for the place where value is created and where problems truly exist. In manufacturing it is the shop floor. In warehousing it is the warehouse. In supply chains it is where materials and information flow.

Our name is our method: understand the problem by being present where the work happens, and build the answer from facts, observation and real operating needs.

The Gemba Approach

Our four-step method. Always written in this order, with these names. We stay from diagnosis through implementation until the new way of working becomes the way the operation runs.

01

Diagnose

Find where performance is actually being lost, and confirm the real constraint.

02

Design

Build the better way of working around the constraint: process, roles, flow and controls.

03

Deploy

Run the change with the team, in real operating conditions, building capability as you go.

04

Durability

Stay until it holds, with the ownership and review rhythms that keep it that way.

Anatomy

Monogram

An interlocking G (Logo Green) and C (Logo Sky). The two letters are one shape. Never separate, re-space or animate them apart.

Wordmark

GEMBA CONCEPTS in capitals, Logo Green, centred under the monogram. It is artwork, not typing: never re-set it in Poppins or any other font.

™ symbol

Stays top-right of the C at its supplied size. Switch to ® only when Legal confirms registration, and update the master files once.

Colour organizes. It never decorates.

Case and special styles

Sentence case

All headlines, titles, buttons and ad headlines. Capitalise only the first word and proper nouns: "Build improvements the operation can keep compounding."

08 — Imagery & illustration

Real work, real floors, real people.

Every image must show the exact operation the words are about. A manufacturing-line post shows a manufacturing line, not a pick-and-dispatch line. Small details have to make sense.

Style 1 · Digital twin

3D isometric operation model

A dark, desaturated model of the plant or warehouse, soft green ambient light, and floating zone labels in dark green tags ("SORTER LINE", "CONTROL ROOM"). Populated with people, vehicles, racks and material at believable scale.

Use for website heroes, campaign key visuals, video, event screens.

Style 2 · Process illustration

Green monochrome scene

A single-hue Gemba green illustration of the exact process on a white or pale green ground, with three to five process icons in circles linked by dotted arrows that tell the story left to right.

Use for blog covers, educational LinkedIn posts, Substack and Medium headers.

Style 3 · Shop-floor photography

Documentary, industry-exact

Real plants, real equipment, correct PPE. Crop to one clear subject. For print collateral, convert to black and white and let green be the only colour on the page.

Use for case studies, brochures, proposals, recruitment.

Style 4 · Product UI

Real screens, real frames

Actual Gemba Connect, MTS and Kairous screens in clean device frames, on Void or Paper. Replace client names and figures with approved demo data.

Use for Platform and Product pages, demos, app-store and product ads.

Rules for every image

Do

Match the subject exactly: the industry, the machine, the material, the PPE, the signage.

A pharma post shows a pharma suite, a cable post shows extrusion or drawing lines.
Do

Make scenes feel premium and human: populated, lit with care, with believable detail.

Flat, empty or purely technical diagrams read as basic.
Do

Put text over images only with the hero scrim, text on the dark side, left-aligned.

See the approved gradients in chapter 04.
Don't

Use generic stock: laptops showing charts, handshakes, smiling office teams, servers, circuit boards.

They say "software vendor", not "we were on your floor".
Don't

Publish anything with an AI look: warped hands or text, plastic skin, impossible machines, nonsense signage.

AI drafts are allowed only if they pass a detail check by a second person.
Don't

Generate or fake people in testimonials, team or "our engineers" visuals.

People shown as Gemba or clients must be real and consented.

Six writing principles

01

Start from the floor

Open with what a plant head sees, not what we sell. "Breakdowns repeat. Production keeps reacting."

02

Name the constraint

Changeovers, micro-stops, waiting, WIP, OTIF, OEE. Never "inefficiencies" or "challenges" on their own.

03

Prove it with a recorded number

"1,404 to 1,923 modules per day in six months." Name the industry and the period. Round only when the source does.

04

Promise execution, not advice

We implement, stabilize and transfer capability. "Built on the floor. Not in a slide deck."

05

Keep the rhythm short

Declarative sentences with full stops, often in pairs or threes. "Find the loss. Fix the system. Make performance repeatable."

06

Ask questions, not sales scripts

"A short note is enough to start. We'll come back with questions, not a sales script."

17 — Accessibility

If it can't be read, it didn't ship.

Plant heads read on phones, on the floor, in bright light. Accessible design is how the message survives that.

Contrast

AA minimum everywhere: 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for text 24px and up. Use the pairs table in chapter 04. White on Green 500 is large text only.

Size

Never below the smallest sizes in chapter 05: 12px web, 22px on a 1080px social canvas, 7.5pt print.

Not colour alone

Status always pairs colour with a word or icon: "Loss −14%" in red, never a red bar alone.

Alt text

Describe the operation: "Operator checking strings at a solar module stringer". Not "image" or "factory".

Captions and transcripts

Every video gets captions. Every podcast episode gets a transcript or detailed show notes.

Documents and motion

PDFs exported with real headings and reading order. Animations respect reduced-motion settings and never flash.
